# Vectorial Capacity & Reproduction Number Explorer

An interactive Shiny application for exploring the classical Garrett-Jones / Ross-Macdonald framework of malaria transmission, with particular emphasis on vectorial capacity, mosquito bionomics, and transmission thresholds.

## Overview

This is an **educational sensitivity explorer**, not a fitted malaria-transmission model. It allows users to manipulate entomological and epidemiological parameters and examine how they affect the classical vectorial-capacity expression and a corresponding scenario reproduction number.

The application deliberately implements a simplified historical framework. It does *not* estimate real-world intervention effect sizes, incidence, prevalence, or the effective reproduction number from surveillance data.

## Scope

### What This App Does

- Visualizes classical vectorial capacity as a function of vector density
- Calculates a scenario reproduction number, $R = C \times b \times c \times D$
- Identifies the $R = 1$ transmission threshold
- Calculates critical vector density and critical human biting rate (HBR)
- Compares illustrative parameter scenarios inspired by common malaria-control mechanisms
- Shows vector survivorship under constant exponential mortality

### What This App Does Not Do

- Estimate empirical $R_0$, $R_C$, or $R_e$ from data
- Model malaria incidence or prevalence over time
- Account for spatial heterogeneity or seasonality
- Represent immunity, superinfection, drug resistance, or host heterogeneity mechanistically
- Represent intervention efficacy mechanistically
- Couple adult mortality to mosquito recruitment or larval density dependence
- Include vector or host age structure

## Biological and Mathematical Background

### Vectorial Capacity ($C$)

The explorer uses the classical Garrett-Jones form:

$$
C = \frac{ma^2p^n}{\mu}
$$

where:

- **$m$**: adult vector density, in mosquitoes per human
- **$a$**: human blood-feeding rate per mosquito per day
- **$p$**: daily probability of mosquito survival
- **$n$**: parasite extrinsic incubation period (EIP), in days
- **$\mu$**: constant mosquito mortality hazard, with $\mu = -\ln(p)$ per day

Under this formulation, $C$ has units of day⁻¹. Conceptually, it is the expected number of potentially infectious mosquito bites that would eventually arise from mosquitoes feeding on one fully infectious human during one day, under the model assumptions.

This is *not* the entomological inoculation rate (EIR). EIR is the number of infectious bites actually received by a person per unit time in an infected population; vectorial capacity instead describes transmission potential generated by a fully infectious human.

The factors have the familiar classical interpretation:

- $m$: how many mosquitoes are available per human
- $a^2$: human feeding appears twice in the human → mosquito → human transmission cycle
- $p^n$: probability that an infected mosquito survives the EIP
- $\frac{1}{\mu}$: expected remaining lifespan under constant exponential mortality

### Human blood feeding and HBR

The explorer derives $a$ from:

$$
a = \frac{h}{g}
$$

where:

- **$h$** is the effective probability that the blood meal associated with a gonotrophic cycle is successfully taken from a human
- **$g$** is the gonotrophic-cycle duration in days

Thus $a$ is a **per-mosquito human blood-feeding rate**, not the human biting rate itself.

The **human biting rate** (HBR) is:

$$
\mathrm{HBR} = ma
$$

with units of bites per human per day.

This derivation assumes one successful blood meal per gonotrophic cycle and does not represent interrupted or repeated feeding attempts.

### Scenario reproduction number ($R$)

The explorer calculates:

$$
R = C \times b \times c \times D
$$

where:

- **$b$**: probability that an infectious mosquito bite infects a susceptible human
- **$c$**: probability that a mosquito becomes infected after biting an infectious human
- **$D$**: effective duration of human infectiousness to mosquitoes, in days

The generic symbol **$R$** is intentional.

- When the parameters describe an **uncontrolled system with a fully susceptible human population**, this quantity corresponds to the classical basic reproduction number **$R_0$**.
- When control measures alter the transmission parameters while the population is otherwise treated as susceptible, the analogous quantity can be interpreted as a **controlled reproduction number ($R_C$)**.
- In immunity-inspired scenarios used here, the calculated value should be interpreted only as a **scenario reproduction number**, because immunity is not modelled dynamically.
- The application does *not* calculate the time-varying effective reproduction number $R_e$.

### Threshold principle

Within this deterministic framework:

- **$R < 1$**: infections decline near the disease-free state
- **$R = 1$**: critical transmission threshold
- **$R > 1$**: sustained transmission is possible under the model assumptions

$R = 1$ should not be confused with a general endemic equilibrium condition. In an endemic system, a time-varying effective reproduction number may approach 1 through depletion of susceptibility, immunity, control, or other processes that are outside this model.

### A note on reproduction-number conventions

This explorer follows the classical Macdonald convention in which one pathogen generation comprises the complete human → mosquito → human cycle, giving $R = CbcD$. Some next-generation-matrix formulations count host and mosquito infection events as separate generations and therefore report the square root of the corresponding full-cycle quantity. The numerical definitions differ, but the critical threshold remains 1.

### Vector mortality

The model assumes a constant mortality hazard:

- Survival function: $S(t) = p^t$
- Mortality hazard: $\mu = -\ln(p)$
- Life expectancy at emergence: $E[T] = \frac{1}{\mu}$
- Expected infectious life per mosquito at emergence: $\frac{p^n}{\mu}$
- Conditional remaining life after surviving the EIP: $\frac{1}{\mu}$

Real mosquito mortality may be age-dependent. The exponential model is retained here because it is central to the classical vectorial-capacity formulation and useful for teaching its sensitivity structure.

## Parameters

### Vector and parasite parameters

- **$p$** (0.5–0.99): daily mosquito survival probability
- **$h$** (0–1): effective probability of a successful human blood meal per gonotrophic cycle
- **$g$** (1–6 days): gonotrophic-cycle duration
- **$n$** (8–50 days): parasite extrinsic incubation period

### Human and infection parameters

- **$D$** (0–200 days): effective duration of human infectiousness to mosquitoes
- **$b$** (0–1): mosquito → human transmission probability per infectious bite
- **$c$** (0–1): human → mosquito infection probability per bite

The default value $D = 80$ days is retained as a historical teaching value associated with classical Macdonald-era malaria theory. It should not be interpreted as a universal contemporary estimate of the duration of *Plasmodium falciparum* infectiousness to mosquitoes. Gametocyte carriage, gametocyte density, and actual mosquito infectivity are not interchangeable quantities.

### Current scenario

- **$m$** (0–200): current adult mosquito density, in mosquitoes per human

## Illustrative Presets

The five presets are *teaching examples only*. They are not empirical estimates of intervention efficacy and should not be used for prediction or policy evaluation.

1. **Illustrative high-transmission baseline**: a highly anthropophagic, long-lived vector population with high density
2. **LLIN-inspired**: lower effective human feeding, lower survival, and lower vector density
3. **IRS-inspired**: substantially lower adult survival and lower vector density in an endophilic vector population
4. **Host-immunity-inspired**: reduced host susceptibility and infectiousness, represented phenomenologically through $b$, $c$, and $D$
5. **Vector-density suppression**: strong reduction in $m$ without specifying whether suppression arises from larval control, genetic control, environmental management, or another mechanism

Because the model treats $m$, $p$, and the feeding parameters as independent controls, it does not represent ecological feedbacks such as increased adult mortality reducing subsequent mosquito recruitment.

## Outputs

### Vectorial-capacity plot

Shows $C(m)$ as a function of mosquito density, with the $R = 1$ threshold implied by the selected values of $b$, $c$, and $D$.

### Key metrics

- Critical mosquito density for $R = 1$
- Critical human biting rate ($ma$) for $R = 1$
- Expected infectious life per mosquito at emergence
- Expected lifespan at emergence
- Current vectorial capacity $C$
- Current HBR ($ma$)
- Current scenario reproduction number $R$

## Insights From the Classical Formulation

Within this particular model—not as universal empirical statements:

- Mosquito survival can have a very large effect because it acts through both survival through the EIP and expected post-EIP lifespan.
- Human blood-feeding rate $a$ enters quadratically.
- Adult mosquito density $m$ has a linear proportional effect when all other parameters are held fixed.
- Effective human infectious duration $D$ has a linear effect.
- EIP $n$ acts nonlinearly through $p^n$.
- Interventions acting on several parameters can combine strongly, but the presets do not estimate real intervention effect sizes.

## Caveats and Contemporary Context

The classical expression is useful precisely because it is compact, but vectorial capacity is model-dependent. Important processes omitted here include:

- age-dependent mosquito mortality and competence
- explicit adult emergence and mosquito population dynamics
- larval density dependence
- seasonality and environmental forcing
- spatial heterogeneity and heterogeneous biting
- multiple vector species
- heterogeneous host infectiousness and susceptibility
- acquired immunity and superinfection
- transient intervention effects and behavioural responses

Modern formulations often expose some of these processes explicitly. Version 1.0 intentionally retains the classical model as a transparent educational reference rather than attempting to combine incompatible extensions into a single calculator.
